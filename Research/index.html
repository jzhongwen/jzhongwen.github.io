Research

I focus my research on the elevational variation in physiological traits of ectotherms and how the variation affects their response to global climate change. Through conducting physiological and ecological experiments and utilizing mechanistic models and species distribution models, I am dedicated to addressing crucial issues in the fields of ecology, evolution and conservation biology.

THE EFFECTS OF HYPOXIA

(Jiang 2021 Biology Letters)

Species was demonstrated to be shifting own range to higher elevation regions in response to the global warming, however, the effects of hypoxia at higher elevation on species remains unclear. We treated two elevational populations (2600 m asl & 3600 m asl) of Phrynocephalus vlangalii under hypoxia (simulating the oxygen level of elevation regions with 1000m upslope-shifting; 3600 m asl & 4600 m asl).


Figure 1 Body temperatures of the Qinghai toad-headed lizard (Phrynocephalus vlangalii) from two elevations (a) 2600 m above sea level (asl), and (b) 3600 m asl) exposed to control and hypoxia treatments. Body temperatures were recorded hourly from 09.00 to 19.00 h. Error bars represent the SE. The body temperature is only comparable between control and hypoxia treatments, but not between the lizards from different elevations because they were maintained at different thermal environments.

The Tpref did not differ between the control and hypoxia treatments in lizards from 2600 m. However, lizards from 3600 m selected lower body temperatures when exposed to the hypoxia treatment mimicking the O2 level at 4600 m (Figure 1).


Figure S1 Critical thermal minimum (CTmin, A) and maximum (CTmax, B) of Qinghai toad-headed lizards (Phrynocephalus vlangalii) from two elevations (2600 m and 3600 m asl), exposed to control and hypoxia treatments. The horizontal line in each box denotes the median, and the top and bottom edges of the box indicate the 75th and 25th percentiles, respectively. Whiskers above and below the box represent the 90th and 10th percentiles, correspondingly. Solid points beyond the whiskers indicate outliers, and the hollow points represent each sample.

Additionally, the hypoxia treatment induced lower critical thermal minimum (CTmin) in lizards from both populations, but did not affect the critical thermal maximum (CTmax) in either population (Figure S1).

Our results imply that upwards-shifting reptiles may be constrained by hypoxia if a decrease in Tpref reduces thermally dependent fitness traits, despite no observed effect on their heat tolerance.
